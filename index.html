    Known limitations

    • Single source. All training images come from one public dataset. Performance on other cameras, populations or illumination is unknown.
    • Shortcuts in the data. In SLID, image resolution and even the cornea annotation shape are associated with the diagnosis. The model may use acquisition cues that do not carry over to new images.
    • Weak lesions. Sensitivity at 90% specificity is below 55% for pinguecula and corneal scarring, and the evidence maps for pinguecula often miss the lesion.
    • Coarse maps. Evidence maps come from a 12 × 9 feature grid. They show the region, not the lesion border.
    • Not a diagnosis. This tool is for research and teaching. It must not guide the care of any patient.

    How it works

    1. Prepare. The image is resized to 384 × 288 pixels in your browser, as in training. Images far from the 4:3 shape are centre-cropped first.
    2. Encode. An ImageNet EfficientNet-B0, kept frozen, turns the image into 1,280 feature maps.
    3. Score. Fourteen linear heads, one for screening and one per lesion, score the averaged features.
    4. Explain. Because the heads are linear, each head applied to every map location gives an exact class activation map.
    5. Check. The image features are compared with the training data. A low similarity triggers a warning.
